title: "Greater Manchester Ringway Trail"
date: 2026-04-26
---
On Friday, I began my section hike of the greater Manchester ringway trail. This is a 200-mile trail that encircles Greater Manchester, beginning and ending in the city centre. It follows canals and rivers and aims to cover a lot of the city without resorting to walking on the roads. It is split into 4 main parts, South, East, North and West, each of which has 5 sections. I thought it would be a really nice way to see the city before I graduate in July, and hopefully give me some motivation to keep exercising during the exam period ahead - I love having something to finish, and I'm on a time limit!

The first section starts at St Peter's Square in Manchester city centre. It's near Manchester Town Hall, which has been under construction the whole time I've been at uni. I think it's supposed to be finished in 2026 though, and they've started taking down some of the scaffolding. I am hoping that by the time I finish my walk and am back at St Peter's Square, it'll be finished. It would be lovely to see what it looks like under there. 
After this, you walk to the Bridgewater Hall (which I don't go to nearly enough) and drop down onto the Rochdale canal. It turns into the Bridgewater canal almost immediately, then there is a lot of canal walking. It follows the tram line too which is cool; the trams don't go near uni or Rusholme so you rarely have a reason to take it as a student. It's cool that they're used though. Last year, whilst I was in the States, they painted all the buses yellow and united them with the trams to form the Bee Network, so Manchester has this great cohesive look. The buses, the old buildings and the rain look pretty iconic together. Anyway, the first bit through Deansgate way was nice. It was also a lovely sunny day which helped. I walked past a lot of people on their way to work; there's a lot of accomodation beside the canal so I guess it gets a lot of foot traffic. After a while you hit Old Trafford, which I was not expecting for some reason (I promise I looked at the map beforehand!). It's a cool thing to see actually - there's not much reason to go to that part of town normally. It was quite dull for a while after that until you get to Stretford. You come off the canal here and walk through some residential areas. Again, the parts of Manchester I'm used to are all student housing etc, so a nice area of town is reasonably unfamiliar. It takes you through Longford Park, which was just very pleasant, and into Sale water park through the Chorlton Ees bit. An 'ee' is an archaic English term for a piece of land which is liable to flood, fyi. Then you walk to the tram stop and hop onto the tram to Firswood and get the bus back from there. 

Overall, a lovely day out and definitely motivated me to carry on doing it. I almost fell asleep in my Early Universe lecture though - a nice 12km walk on a sunny day followed by 2hrs of talking about inflation will do that to you! Perhaps I will update more on here, it's a great way to procrastinate writing my MPhys report. The next section is from Sale to Didsbury through parks, I think it'll be lovely.
