In the last month, I've completed a further 4 sections of the GM ringway trail. I thought it would be fun to do a little recap of them here, since I described the first one in such detail. 


Section 2: Sale to Didsbury


I did this section on the 29th April. The day had started with a presentation I gave at the 'physics undergraduate conference', at which there were very few attendees. In fact, at the beginning a solid half of my audience were guys from orchestra who don't have an interest in physics and just came to support me, which was nice. After this I did some minimal coding for my C++ assignment, and met my friends after their lecture to walk home together. It was, however, a beautiful day, and this quickly devolved into the three of us having a beer outside the student's union. Whoops! I was planning on walking on this day anyway, and since we'd had a beer I decided it was in my best interests to walk now, work later. So I got on a bus to a tram stop, and on a tram to Sale. 


It was about 3pm at this point, so I ran into all the schoolkids on their way home. This was a little annoying, but I got there in the end. After this, the walk was delightful. It follows along the Mersey to Chorlton water park, where there were children playing, a man strumming his guitar, many people out on walks and so on and so forth. I had to miss a section through the woods since there appears to be a bridge that is currently out of commission (i.e. non-existent). This was okay though, the banks of the Mersey are very pleasant to stroll down. The perfectionist in me may one day return for this tiny section. At one point it cuts through a golf course which was a bit stressful, I dislike being near where people are playing ball sports! Not long afterwards you reach Fletcher Moss botanical gardens. These are absolutely gorgeous and I can't believe I never visited before! They also have these really neat benches that have been carved into different sculptures. You come out of here next to a number of pubs; had I been accompanied it may have led to a visit. I did come back here on my mother's birthday a few weeks later to see the gardens again and go for a meal, would certainly recommend. Then you just walk through Didsbury until you reach the tram stop. I did this and then immediately traced my footsteps back to reach the bus stop, as trams do not go to my part of town, and proceeded to wait for several minutes until a nice gentleman informed me that the buses were rerouted at that time and so I should walk further up the road. Nice of him to let me know! All in all, this was a delightful day.


Section 3: Didsbury to Bramhall


I completed this section on the 3rd of May. My immediate recollection of this section is that it was not as nice as the previous ones. This had to happen at some point, since the others were fantastic. It was more of a grey day, and I think I'd had somewhat of an unproductive morning. Walking tends to make me work better overall so this was a bit of a tactical move to get the gears turning. It was much easier to get there than the last section, since it is simply a bus down to Didsbury. You start off following the tram line for some time, before Parrs Wood where you get some forested space, and you come back down and across the Mersey. Unfortunately this amount of walking near the Mersey necessarily means the song gets stuck in your head, as it is in mine as I write, as I hope it is in yours now. :D. You then walk down a big road and across a motorway, before heading into Abney Hall Park. I liked it here, there was an old people's home, cows, and a large pond. You then walk through Cheadle.


Something I've really enjoyed about the trail is the signs. The most common ones are a square QR code with a green triangle on top, forming an arrow that points you in the right direction. There are also large dark green ones with a white arrow and black text. Also, you have white ones with a green arrow, where the green arrow contains a little outline of the trail! Some of them are just stickers on lampposts while others are more traditional signs on posts, but it's fun that there's the variety, and also nice to think about the people who have been there before you, putting the signs up. Anyway,


You very much just walk down the high street in Cheadle. It is not that special and very similar to any other big street in an English town. After this you sort of turn back on yourself and follow Micker Brook for a while. This was nice, all through parks, although there was a lot of work going on so it didn't look as beautiful as I'm sure it normally does. The work seems to be oriented towards keeping the brook clean, though, so I'm not annoyed by it. You carry on following it until Micker Brook ends and Lady Brook begins. You're supposed to follow Lady Brook for longer but I was thwarted by some more work in the woods with large fences protecting it, meaning I had to detour through some more suburban areas. Alas! You then go through Bramhall Park, which has a really cool looking hall/chapel thing - also under construction. I would like to see it once it's done though. Then you follow a path through the woods to the train station. I got the bus though, since trains weren't running, and I managed to read my friend's entire dissertation while waiting for one to arrive. We had a fascinating conversation over text about the use of genAI in undergraduate degrees and compared our experiences in psychology vs physics. Not that this was overly relevant to the walk, it just took me a really long time to get home. Her dissertation was fantastic if you were wondering. 


Writing this has made me realise that this section was cooler than I remember it being! It's neat how each one is so different and memorable.


Section 4: Bramhall to Middlewood


I did this section on the 22nd May. This was my nominal Day Off From Exams, and the UK is experiencing a heatwave, so I had to go on a walk. The trains were running to Bramhall again, so I opted to do that instead of taking the bus - it was faster for me to go into the city centre by bus and back out again via train instead. However, I had a dilemma. Trains back from Middlewood go once every two hours, so I had to either do the walk in 2 hours or fill my time for 4. I could possibly have chosen a different arrival time to avoid this problem but I was more focused on getting outdoors whenever I woke up while not compromising on sleep. Anyway, the walk was 13 km which would typically take me 2.5 hours. To combat this timing issue I decided to just run half of it. This was great, and also emulated my favourite hobby - skipping my lectures to go on a run somewhere random, although this time I wasn't skipping anything but revision. 


The first part of the walk took me all the way back through Bramhall Park, only after the chapel thing I turned in the opposite direction to which I had approached it. I ran this bit since it was shaded and I'd seen it all before. You then go through the 'happy valley', which I thought was very cute. You're still following Lady Brook here. I like the focus of the ringway on waterways; I suppose it makes sense that the more trail-y bits through the city would be by rivers/brooks/canals. You then go through more of a field, losing the trees (not that nice in the heat!) and meet a road, from which you turn onto another road. This was one of those roads which is more like a driveway for a lot of people, if that makes sense; it's narrow and you sort of feel like you shouldn't be there. There are lots of these on the ringway. However, as they all do, it turned into a footpath which was nicer. This takes you eventually to Poynton, which seems very posh. You take the long way round a lovely lake. The first side was very forest-y and quiet. I saw a magpie eating a large frog. That was a first. Then you reach some nice-looking houses, and turn back on yourself, and that side of the lake is more well-paved and has lots of people walking around, it was a strange disparity between the two. Then there's some more road, some more track, and eventually some fields. I ran through these again; I've seen plenty of fields before. It's also really nice having the option to run but not the pressure, it felt like a true frolic more so than either a run or a hike. After this, there was a farm that seemed to be geared towards weddings/events, it looked nice but expensive and had some very vocal lambs! There was some more road after this but not with pavement, instead 60mph road with a bank on one side and a fence on the other. Less than ideal. I took a random detour off of it since there were lots of cars and ended up climbing very high and then quickly back down to the road. I then, again, simply ran the rest of the road to get it done more quickly. Middlewood train station is then sort of just right there in the middle of nowhere. Or in the middle of the woods, I guess. Oh. Right. Then I showered and went to the pub :D.


Section 5: Middlewood to Strines


I completed this section today, on the 28th of May, with my friend Frances. We again got the train down together. I just passed my MPhys thesis defence, which I was very proud of after a long semester of not having anything in the lab work, so a walk was in order. Frances has finished her degree and so is no longer bound by essay writing, so she's free to come and hang out when I leave my so-called 'physics jail'. Also, we were both in a hiking club in the US last year, so it was a fun reminder of that to go hiking together again. The trail follows the Middlewood Way for a bit, before diverging. You walk through the woods to the Macclesfield Canal and then cross, past some lovely houseboats, and follow Bollinhurst Brook for a bit. This looks like it would be a lovely spot if one wanted to spend the day hanging out near some water, it was very quiet and shaded. After this there was a section of trail that had some warning signs about rattlesnakes; a little nervewracking but nobody got bitten! It was a proper countryside walk, which I really enjoyed, it's lovely to see that side of Manchester. Eventually we came upon the National Trust property Lyme Park. It took us through the woods to get to the actual estate, which was full of young families enjoying the sun. We got an ice cream here - it was en route and felt necessary - before starting a climb. Not a massive one, but I probably won't climb a hill eating ice cream again! Up here we got a great view of Manchester and the surrounding countryside. I'd recommend it if you wanted some views for minimal effort. The rest of the property looked interesting too, unfortunately we couldn't explore much but I would certainly return. 


Leaving Lyme Park, we hit a big road, which was stressful to cross! We did in the end, though, and got hit with another climb; fortunately this one was shaded and had lots of pretty flowers. It took us to some more civilization and road walking, thankfully with pavements. We turned off and went through a field. Once we reached the top of the field, the way to go was not quite apparent - in the end it was between a fence and a wall hedge, and *very* overgrown. We came out of this bit with many nettle stings between the two of us. Then, we followed more of a road, going up, back on ourselves, and down to the Peak Forest Canal. We followed this for a while. It has some very beautiful houses and canal boats. We walked past a few people, including a little girl who was joyous about playing in a bucket, and a few goslings! We turned off and then it was a straight line to Strines with a lot of downhill to the River Goyt. Notable highlights included a pond with some cute flowers, and Bruce's Clock. It is called this because a man named Bruce built it, as we learnt from the sign beneath. Strines station is then another bit of a climb, which was not so nice in the heat. It was a lovely section though, I enjoyed going with a friend. 


I am hoping to do the next section on June 9th, since now I must revise and stop walking around so much! Then I will have around 3 weeks to attempt to complete 15 sections; if not I will try to squeeze some in when I come back to graduate. I'm very convinced that I can do it though!
